# RDRS Bangladesh — Interview and Field Notes (for MIS internship report)

## Who I spoke with

I spoke with three people at RDRS during my internship.

First was Md. Saiful Alam (Shekul). He is now on the ICPS project baseline team. Before RDRS he worked as Technical Specialist, MEAL and GIS on the BHA project with Save the Children in Sylhet. We talked for close to two hours. He walked me through what a MEL department actually does, how a baseline is designed, and what indicators, ESS, TOR and Inception Report mean in practice.

Second was Fahmida Mostafa, Senior MEL Officer. She explained the active projects, what the MEL department handles day to day, and how reporting moves from the field to MIS. She also gave me the figures on RDRS scale and the five programme areas.

Third was Mr. Iftekharul Islam, Manager, Monitoring. He told me how microfinance MIS changed at RDRS, from manual work to the current system.

I also went through handwritten notes and four supporting documents: the September 2026 monitoring schedule, sample MIS reports from the current system, the strategic plan, and the May 2023 field monitoring report on the Somriddhi programme.

## RDRS in brief

RDRS started in February 1972 as the Bangladesh field programme of the Lutheran World Federation in Geneva. It did relief and rehabilitation work in the northwest after the war. In 1997 it registered under the Trust Act as a Bangladeshi NGO. It is now running its sixth Strategic Plan, 2023 to 2027. Head Office is in Dhaka, with a Central Coordination Office in Rangpur.

Vision: A just and peaceful society where citizens live in dignity, free from poverty, distress, and ignorance in a sustainable environment.

Mission: RDRS works with disadvantaged and marginalized communities and their organizations to empower them, make them resilient, and ensure sustainable improvements in their lives with dignity.

Core values listed now are Compassion, Commitment, Innovation, Adaptability, Accountability.

Fahmida gave me the current field scale. 406 Federations. 33 districts. 183 upazilas. 872 unions. About 16,42,724 people directly involved, roughly 1.64 million.

Most work is in Rangpur Division: Kurigram, Gaibandha, Thakurgaon, Dinajpur, Nilphamari, Panchagarh, Rangpur, Lalmonirhat. There is also work in Cox's Bazar, in Ukhiya and Teknaf, with Rohingya refugees and host communities. A smaller footprint in Sylhet, Mymensingh and Rajshahi divisions.

RDRS groups its work into five areas:
1. Skills, Employment and Entrepreneurship
2. Social Empowerment
3. Agriculture, Food Security and Climate Change
4. Microfinance and Employment Creation
5. Emergency and Humanitarian Response

Monitoring Officers cover all five. Each officer supports monitoring across them.

Four projects came up repeatedly in conversation:

Jononni works with pregnant women. The formal name in documents looks like Strengthening the Maternal and Neonatal Health System in Rangpur, with Save the Children, March 2023 to December 2027. It was on the September 2026 monitoring schedule, so it is active.

SEEDS stands for Socio-Economic Empowerment with Dignity and Sustainability. It works with adolescents in adibashi communities. Fahmida said women there carry most household work, so adolescents get little time to study. SEEDS supports their schooling. Funded by Stromme Foundation, January 2024 to December 2028, for 3,000 families.

MCGL is the MOMENTUM Country and Global Leadership Program in Bangladesh. Funded by the U.S. Department of State through Jhpiego. Focus is fewer preventable deaths and better maternal, newborn and child health.

## How the MEL department works

MEL means Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning. Saiful used to use MEAL at Save the Children, where the extra A is Accountability to beneficiaries. At RDRS the department is called MEL.

The daily flow Fahmida described is simple enough.

MEL staff go to project sites, watch activities, and collect data. For regular reports they use structured Excel formats and registers. For one-off needs they use KoBo Toolbox on mobile. KoBo lets them change questions quickly and export straight to Excel. Back in office they compile in Excel. Power BI is used for some dashboards. Management then uses those MEL outputs in MIS for decisions.

The September 2026 monitoring schedule shows how this turns into monthly assignments. It has two parts.

The microfinance part lists ten repeat tasks, each assigned to one person and one branch, visited twice a month, 02 to 15 September and 16 to 28 September. The tasks are loan and savings verification at group level, staff productivity checks, watching savings collection and refunds, checking loan disbursement papers, following up loan ceilings and overdue loans, following up write-offs and rescheduled loans, checking audit and reconciliation, looking into write-off cases, checking misappropriation adjustments, and assessing pre-service training.

The development part is project specific. That month it included baseline for the C-Rangpur project in Rangpur, Lalmonirhat, Kurigram, Sirajganj, Gaibandha and Jamalpur; DHIS2 validation and process checks for MCGL in Panchagarh; DQA plus ELCO and PW listing and result checks for Jononni in Lalmonirhat and Rangpur; economic inclusion and education checks for SEEDS in Rangpur; baseline for ICPS in Mymensingh, Rajshahi and Rangpur Division from 06 to 24 September, assigned to Saiful Alam Sekul and Fahmida Mostafa; post-distribution follow-up for RERP IGA beneficiaries in Cox's Bazar; and beneficiary selection checks for CALDRI in Kurigram.

I am noting the ICPS line because that is the same baseline Saiful explained to me. It was live while I was there.

## Baseline design, learned through ICPS

ICPS full title is Strengthening Disability-Inclusive Child Protection Systems to Prevent and Respond to Violence against Children with Disabilities in Selected Communities of Bangladesh. Donor is UNICEF. Period June 2026 to December 2027. Budget BDT 2,20,40,834. It covers Output 4.2 on social service systems and Output 4.3 on harmful practices. Baseline survey was running in September 2026.

A few terms kept coming up, so I wrote them down plainly.

ESS is Environmental and Social Safeguard. Projects have to follow it for environmental and social risks.

UNICEF gave 21 indicators for this baseline. Qualitative ones are collected through FGD and KII. Quantitative ones come from household survey.

Baseline itself just means measuring the target group before the project starts, against those indicators. That measurement becomes the reference for later progress.

MEL maturity was a new phrase for me. It means how systematic and joined-up an organization's M and E work is. More mature means more regular and evidence based.

TOR and Inception Report come at the start. TOR sets scope and method for the baseline. Inception Report confirms method and work plan before fieldwork.

Saiful listed baseline design in six steps. Review project document. Select indicators. Build the indicator and methodology matrix. Draft the baseline concept. Write the TOR. Write the Inception Report. His notes ticked them in order, up to Inception, which fits with fieldwork starting in September.

Method split was short. FGD and KII for qualitative. Household survey for quantitative.

## How microfinance MIS changed

This part came from Mr. Iftekharul Islam. It stuck with me because my family is in it.

In 1995 to 1997 RDRS microfinance was starting. The field role then was called ATM, Assistant Thana Manager. First software was called Micro Bank, built with support from FAO and GTZ from Germany. My notes say ZTZ, but I think that means GTZ. A team of 27 developers worked on it. My grandfather, Boro Abbu, was one of them, with a colleague noted as Mahabub.

After that my grandfather ran most MIS reporting on Micro Bank himself. Over time RDRS leaned on him too much for reports. One person holding that much reporting knowledge is risky. An organization that size cannot run that way for long.

In 2011 RDRS brought in South Tech to reduce that dependence. My grandfather helped set it up, then retired.

Around 2017 to 2018 RDRS moved to G Banker, a Bangladeshi product. That is the current system. It pulls data together into an integrated dashboard. Routine branch reports now come out with one click.

I saw three outputs from G Banker.

One was the Micro Finance Program Changes Report, CO-wise. The example I saw was Udayan office. It shows month to month change by CO: CO count, samity count, member balance, savings, loanee numbers, overdue, outstanding, OTR and PAR, profit and loss, disbursement, write-offs.

The other two were POMIS-5, Statement of Financial Position, which is a branch balance sheet comparing this year to last year; and POMIS-4, Statement of Comprehensive Income and Expenditure, a branch income statement, monthly and annual, straight from the system.

## What a real monitoring report reads like: Somriddhi, May 2023

To see MEL output on paper, I read the May 2023 monitoring report on the Somriddhi Programme. Full Bengali title translates roughly as Increasing Assets and Capacity of Poor Households for Poverty Alleviation. Funded by PKSF.

It covered Vojanpur and Debiduba unions in Panchagarh, where work started in 2014, and Beruabari union in Kurigram, started in 2018.

The visit wanted to check implementation and papers, education centers and student levels, health work, rehabilitated members, and Somriddhi Bari homestead work.

Method was structured questionnaires and FGDs with beneficiaries, with purposive and random sampling. Two of three unions were covered that round. Documents were reviewed too.

Education: 74 centers in total, 34 in Beruabari and 40 in Debiduba, serving 1,926 students. Team visited 12 centers directly. MIS attendance did not match registers. In Beruabari MIS showed 100 percent on average against 89 percent in the register. Pass rates combined were Bangla 82 percent, English 53 percent, Math 61 percent, with big gaps between centers. Teacher turnover was the main problem. 11 of 74 centers changed teacher within four months. Materials were short, no play equipment, and only 45 percent of centers followed lesson plans properly.

Health: health card coverage was 17 percent of households in Beruabari and 38 percent in Debiduba. Static clinics, satellite clinics twice a week, plus special health and eye camps run under the card. Card and diabetes test fees over four months were BDT 47,620 in Beruabari and BDT 91,660 in Debiduba. People in FGDs were mostly positive. They asked for more satellite clinics, lower card cost, and steadier medicine supply.

Rehabilitation: 2018 to 2019 cohort had 4 beneficiaries in Beruabari and 8 in Debiduba, each given about BDT 1 lakh in assets. In Beruabari incomes had mostly fallen. Most beneficiaries are over 70 and could not find ways to reinvest. In Debiduba 60 percent saw income rise. Follow-up was patchy. The team could not find 65 percent of income and expenditure records during the visit. Housing conditions were better since baseline, with more electricity, tubewell and sanitation access.

Homestead: 25 homes in Beruabari and 20 in Debiduba got saplings, vegetable seeds, compost rings, fencing, poultry and pigeon houses, and signboards in 2018 to 2019. Tree survival was low. Fruit trees 35 percent in Beruabari against 49 percent in Debiduba. Flood damage, late planting and weak saplings were blamed. Vermicompost, eco-stoves and pheromone traps were given out but rarely in use.

One pattern ran through the whole report. MIS figures and field registers often did not match. That matters for my topic, because it is the same data integrity issue I saw in loan record matching, just at programme level.

## Terms I had to keep looking up

MEL: Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning.
MEAL: same plus Accountability, used in some INGOs.
ESS: Environmental and Social Safeguard.
TOR: Terms of Reference.
FGD: Focus Group Discussion.
KII: Key Informant Interview.
HH Survey: Household Survey.
IGA: Income Generating Activity.
KoBo Toolbox: mobile data collection app.
PKSF: Palli Karma-Sahayak Foundation.
MFI: Microfinance Institution.
ATM: Assistant Thana Manager, old RDRS field role.
CO: Credit Organizer, samity level field staff.
OTR: On-Time Repayment rate.
PAR: Portfolio at Risk.
DQA: Data Quality Assessment.
